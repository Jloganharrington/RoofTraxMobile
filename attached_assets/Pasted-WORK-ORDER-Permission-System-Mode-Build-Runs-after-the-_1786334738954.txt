STEP 2 — Single resolver
Two entry points in lib/authz, and nothing else may decide authorization:

resolvePermissions(actor) → Set<Permission> — static, for nav, widgets, and the settings UI.
can(actor, permission, resource?) → boolean — resource-aware, for routes. Ownership-scoped permissions need the resource; passing one where it's required is a type error, not a runtime surprise.
resolvePermissions subsumes the existing resolveCapabilities. Keep the widget catalog reading through it so the dashboard doesn't fork.

Unit tests: every permission × every role × relevant department and workflow, plus owner and non-owner for the ownership-scoped ones. This is the safety net for Step 3.

STEP 4 — Reporting tree

Manager deletion authority becomes rank and assignment: a manager may delete only users assigned to them whom they strictly outrank. Admin and above keep pure rank. A manager with assigned reports cannot be deleted until those reports are reassigned — return a clear error naming how many.

STEP 5 — Per-user overrides
user_permission_overrides: company_id, user_id, permission, effect (grant | revoke), granted_by_user_id, granted_at, reason (required).

Resolution order, fixed and documented: default → revoke → grant, with floor permissions winning over everything. A floor permission can never be granted or revoked, at any level.

Three rules the write path must enforce, each with its own test:

You can only grant what you hold. An actor cannot grant a permission absent from their own resolved set.
You can only act on someone you outrank — and for managers, only on their assigned reports, matching Step 4.
Every change is audited. Same shape as pin_financial_changes, reason required.
