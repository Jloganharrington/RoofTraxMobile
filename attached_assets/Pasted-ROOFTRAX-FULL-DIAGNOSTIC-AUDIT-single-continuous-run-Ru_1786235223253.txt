═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
THE ONE RULE THAT MATTERS MOST
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
⚠ NEVER FABRICATE A CONNECTION.

If you find something orphaned — a column nothing reads, a hook nothing
imports, a field nothing populates — and you cannot determine from the code
what it was meant to connect to, you LOG IT AS ORPHANED and move on. Do not
wire it to the most plausible-looking consumer. Do not infer intent from a
name. A wrong connection is far more damaging than a missing one, because a
missing one stays visible and a wrong one looks finished.

Same rule for values: if a number appears wrong, do not "correct" it to what
seems right. Report the discrepancy with both sides cited.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
FIX AUTHORITY — a whitelist, not a judgment call
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
You MAY fix, as you go, ONLY these:

  1. Comments and docstrings that describe behaviour the code does not have.
  2. A hand-written queryKey or invalidateQueries literal replaced with the
     generated get*QueryKey function for that same endpoint.
  3. Unused imports and unreachable local variables.
  4. A customFetch call swapped for the existing generated hook for the SAME
     endpoint, where the hook already exists and the shapes match exactly.
  5. Obvious typos in user-facing strings.

You MAY NOT fix, under any circumstances — log these instead:

  - Anything touching money math, cents conversion, or the profitability view
  - Anything touching authz, role gates, or tenancy scoping
  - Any schema change, migration, or column
  - Deleting ANY code, even if it appears dead
  - Anything in artifacts/mobile — it is mid-build and app-store bound
  - Adding a route, spec entry, or regenerating clients
  - Wiring an orphan to a consumer (see THE ONE RULE)

If a fix looks like it belongs on the allowed list but requires touching
anything on the forbidden list to complete — stop that fix, revert it, and
log it as a finding instead.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
OUTPUT FORMAT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Classify every finding. No item may be classified without evidence, and
cite file:line for both ends of any claimed connection.

  WIRED      Verified end to end. Cite BOTH ends.
  BROKEN     Connection expected but missing, mismatched, or dead.
  ORPHANED   Exists, nothing references it. Cite the search that proved it.
  LEGACY     Superseded. Cite the replacement.
  FIXED      You repaired it. State exactly what changed and why it was on
             the whitelist.
  BLOCKED    Could not be tested or verified because something upstream is
             broken. Name what blocked it, then MOVE ON — do not stall.
  UNCERTAIN  Could not determine. State precisely what you could not
             establish.

⚠ "I searched and found nothing" and "I did not search" are different
findings. Never report the second as the first.

⚠ WRITE FINDINGS TO A FILE AS YOU GO — append after each pass, do not hold
the whole audit in memory until the end. A crash at pass 6 must not lose
passes 1 through 5.
