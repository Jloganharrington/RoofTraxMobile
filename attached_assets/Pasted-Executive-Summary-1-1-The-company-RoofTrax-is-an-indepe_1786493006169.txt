Executive Summary
1.1 The company

RoofTrax is an independent B2B software company serving storm restoration contractors. Its product is a multi-tenant CRM and mobile field inspection platform built around an AI-assisted proof package generation pipeline. The differentiating claim is not workflow management — that market is served — but documentation output quality at rep level.

This document establishes four things:

Who we sell to (ICP and anti-ICP)
What we claim (value proposition and positioning)
What we charge (five-tier hybrid model with annual prepay bias)
How we validate (90-day paid beta with defined exit criteria)

Headline economics: at 350 customers with the recommended tier structure and a 70% annual prepay rate, the model produces approximately $2.8–3.0M effective ARR, against ~$118K/year in delivery COGS. Gross margin is approximately 96%.

1.2 Relationship to NuHome Exteriors, Inc.

RoofTrax and NuHome Exteriors are separate companies. NuHome is a restoration contractor. RoofTrax is a software vendor. They share a founder and nothing else.

The relationship is that of design partner and reference customer: NuHome was the first environment the platform was built against and validated in, and it remains a paying customer under the same commercial terms as any other. It is not a parent company, a division, an affiliate, or an incubator, and RoofTrax is not a NuHome product.

This separation must be maintained across four dimensions:

Dimension	Requirement
Legal	Distinct entity, EIN, bank accounts, contracts, and insurance. NuHome's contractor licenses do not extend to RoofTrax and must never be cited in RoofTrax materials.
Financial	No commingled funds. Any shared service, IP assignment, or founder time allocation documented as an arm's-length agreement with defined consideration.
Data	NuHome is a tenant like any other. No cross-tenant visibility, no RoofTrax access to competitor claim data for NuHome's benefit. This is contractual, not merely technical.
Brand	Independent identity, domain, and collateral. RoofTrax materials do not carry NuHome branding, licenses, or letterhead.

Why this matters commercially, not just legally. RoofTrax's ICP is roofing contractors. If prospects perceive RoofTrax as an arm of a competing contractor, three problems follow immediately: they assume their claim data, pricing, and pipeline are visible to a rival; they discount the neutrality of the product's recommendations; and any contractor within plausible competitive range refuses to evaluate. Vertical SaaS founded by operators is common and generally an asset — but only where the software company is unambiguously its own business.

Data neutrality is the single most likely objection in the sales cycle. Prepare a direct answer: distinct entities, tenant isolation with no cross-tenant access, contractual non-use terms, and a willingness to submit to third-party security review. Address it proactively in the first call rather than waiting for it.

2. Ideal Customer Profile
2.1 Primary ICP — "The Scaling Restoration Shop"
Attribute	Profile
Company size	5–25 employees; 3–15 field sales reps
Annual revenue	$2M–$15M
Claim volume	15–60 insurance claims per month
Business mix	60%+ insurance restoration; may carry retail
Geography	Storm-exposed markets outside the DC/MD/VA metro
Current stack	AccuLynx, JobNimbus, or spreadsheets + Dropbox
Measurement	Already buying EagleView, Hover, or GAF QuickMeasure

The defining pain: the owner or a single supplement manager is the documentation bottleneck. Every proof package, every supplement narrative, and every carrier rebuttal routes through one person's head. Growth is capped by that person's hours, and every new rep hire increases the bottleneck rather than relieving it.

Buying trigger events, in order of strength:

Lost or underpaid a claim they believed was documented adequately
Hired reps and discovered output quality collapsed
Supplement manager quit, or is threatening to
Entering a new state and facing unfamiliar code requirements
Renewal quote arrived from AccuLynx or JobNimbus

Economic buyer: owner or GM. Deals close in one to three calls; this is not an enterprise procurement cycle.

2.2 Secondary ICP — "The Regional Operator"

25–100 employees, $15M–$60M revenue, multi-state, with an established supplement department of 2–5 people. Longer sales cycle (4–8 weeks), higher scrutiny on data security and multi-tenancy, and a real requirement for role-based permissions. Higher ACV, materially lower churn, and the source of case studies that unlock the primary ICP.

Their pain is different: not the absence of a documentation process, but the inconsistency of it across offices and reps. Sell standardization and auditability to this segment, not capability.

2.3 Anti-ICP — Do Not Sell

Explicit disqualifiers. Each one predicts high support cost, high churn, or unacceptable risk:

Public adjusters and PA firms. RoofTrax is architected around contractor-lane discipline. Selling to PAs contaminates the positioning that makes the product defensible.
Contractors in NuHome's service area — a decision, not a rule. See §2.4. Northern Virginia, DC, and suburban Maryland are excluded during beta and Phase 1, but this is a founder-level conflict question rather than a permanent product constraint.
Pure retail exterior contractors. No insurance volume means the core value proposition does not apply; they will churn within two quarters.
Sub-$500K revenue / solo operators without reps. The value proposition is rep-level output. With no reps, there is no leverage to sell.
Anyone seeking claim outcome guarantees. A prospect who asks us to guarantee carrier payment is a prospect who will misuse the product and misrepresent it to homeowners.
Storm chasers with no fixed office. Documentation discipline requires operational permanence.
2.4 The service-area conflict — unresolved

Now that RoofTrax is a standalone company, excluding NoVA/DC/Maryland contractors is no longer automatically correct. The two entities have opposed interests here, and only the founder can resolve it:

RoofTrax's interest: a dense, nearby, high-volume metro is an obvious early market — easy to service in person, easy to build case studies in.
NuHome's interest: every local contractor with RoofTrax documentation is a materially stronger competitor in NuHome's own bidding market.

Three viable postures:

Option	Trade-off
Permanent exclusion	Cleanest optics; forfeits a natural home market. Requires accepting that RoofTrax is structurally handicapped in its own backyard.
Time-limited exclusion	Exclude through Phase 1, revisit at Gate 2. Preserves optionality; requires the decision to actually get made rather than deferred indefinitely.
Open with disclosure	Sell to anyone; disclose the founder's contractor affiliation and rely on tenant isolation plus contractual non-use. Maximizes RoofTrax; accepts real competitive cost to NuHome.

Recommendation: time-limited exclusion through Phase 1. It costs little early — there is plenty of addressable market elsewhere and the beta already requires out-of-area participants for clean feedback — and it defers the decision to a point where RoofTrax's independent viability is proven and the founder's allocation between the two businesses is clearer.

Whatever the choice, disclose the founder's contractor affiliation regardless. A prospect who discovers it independently treats it as concealment; a prospect who hears it in the first call generally treats it as the credential it is.

3. Value Proposition
3.1 Core positioning statement

RoofTrax turns your newest rep into your best documenter.

Your 22-year-old field rep produces a forensic proof package indistinguishable from one written by a 20-year supplement manager — from their phone, at the property, before they leave the driveway.

Everything else in the product is evidence for that sentence.

3.2 The category argument

AccuLynx, JobNimbus, and Roofr are job management systems. They track where a job is. They do not make the job win.

RoofTrax is a documentation and evidence system with job management attached. The proof package is the product; the CRM is the container that makes it repeatable.

This distinction matters commercially: we are not asking a contractor to rip out their CRM on a feature comparison, which is a losing argument against incumbents with a decade of engineering. We are asking them to adopt a documentation capability that no incumbent has, and which happens to include the CRM they were already paying for.

3.3 Value equation

Dream outcome — documentation quality stops depending on which rep showed up.

Perceived likelihood of achievement — this is the strongest asset and it is underused. RoofTrax was not built by a software founder theorizing about restoration. It was built by someone with twenty years in insurance restoration, designed against a real restoration department's daily workload and validated there before a line of it was sold.

How to tell that story now that the entities are separate. The credential is the founder's two decades of restoration and supplement experience — that is what makes the product credible, and it travels with him regardless of corporate structure. NuHome is cited as the design partner and first customer that the platform was built and proven against, named with permission, the same way any vertical SaaS company names its anchor customer.

What the story must not do is blur the entities. RoofTrax is not "NuHome's software." Phrasing to use: "Built by a twenty-year restoration operator, proven in a live restoration department before it was ever sold." Phrasing to avoid: "We're a contractor who built software" — that sentence tells a prospect their data sits inside a competitor.

Time delay — first proof package generated on the customer's live claim within 48 hours of signup. Not a sandbox, not a demo file. Their claim, their property, their carrier.

Effort and sacrifice — migration is performed by RoofTrax, not the customer. Their existing CRM export, price book, document templates, and photo library are loaded for them.

3.6 Claims discipline — hard constraint

RoofTrax marketing must not claim, guarantee, or imply influence over coverage determinations, carrier payment amounts, or claim approval rates.

This is not conservatism. The entire product architecture — contractor-lane discipline, building-as-sentence-subject, no coverage interpretation — exists because that lane is what keeps the output defensible and keeps the company outside public adjusting statutes in every state. Marketing that implies claim influence destroys the asset the product is built on and creates regulatory exposure in states with stricter UPPA regimes than Virginia's.

Permitted claims: documentation quality, completeness, speed, consistency, code citation accuracy, rep-level capability. Prohibited claims: approval rates, recovery amounts, "we get claims paid," settlement outcomes, any customer testimonial phrased as a payment result.

Enforce this in the beta agreement as a condition of case-study participation.

4. Pricing Model
4.1 Structural principles
Committed floor plus metered expansion. Every customer has a monthly minimum. No customer can reach $0 in a slow quarter.
Meter the bottom, uncap the top. At $0.75 COGS per package, unlimited generation at Scale and Enterprise is nearly costless and is a decisive competitive weapon.
Overage priced as an upgrade trigger, not cost recovery. Set so the rational move is to upgrade at roughly 130% of included volume.
Annual prepay is the default path. It funds acquisition, neutralizes storm seasonality, and materially reduces year-one churn.
4.2 Tier structure
Tier	Annual (per mo)	Monthly	Seats	Packages incl.	Overage
Field	$83	$99	3	3 / mo	$40
Starter	$208	$249	8	10 / mo	$40
Pro	$583	$699	20	35 / mo	$25
Scale	$1,416	$1,699	50	Unlimited	—
Enterprise	$2,900+	$3,500+	Custom	Unlimited	—

Annual terms: paid upfront, equivalent to two months free.

Setup and migration fee: $2,500 — waived on annual prepay. Charge it, then give it away. It costs nothing to waive and makes the annual decision self-evident.

Credit banking: included packages bank across the annual term rather than expiring monthly. Storm restoration is violently seasonal; use-it-or-lose-it metering is punitive in this industry and banking is a marketable differentiator against every incumbent.

4.3 Guarantee

The 30-Day Live Claim Guarantee Ten complete, submission-ready proof packages on your real claims within 30 days — or we refund the full year and you keep every package.

This is a deliverable guarantee. It is entirely within RoofTrax's control, sits entirely inside the contractor lane, and reverses the perceived risk of annual prepayment. It is deliberately not an outcome guarantee (see §3.6).

Secondary guarantee: if the customer's reps are not generating packages unassisted by day 30, onboarding support continues free until they are.

4.4 Bonus stack

Included at Pro and above. Each is high perceived value and near-zero marginal cost:

AHJ code library pre-loaded for the customer's jurisdiction
The four-document operating system — PRSIA, CFR Addendum, Kitchen-Table Process Card, Internal SOP
Adjuster negotiation training — recorded Voss/Black Swan methodology module
Live package review — first five packages reviewed by Logan personally

The bonus stack is where the offer is won. Competitors sell software. RoofTrax sells an operating system with software attached.

Package generation is not the cost center. Media storage and support are. Storage in particular compounds — model it as cumulative GB per tenant per year, not as a per-report figure. This is the entire cost structure of CompanyCam and it should be tracked from day one of beta.

5. Beta Program Implementation Plan
5.1 Objectives

The beta is not a marketing exercise. It exists to answer five questions:

Do non-owner users generate proof packages unassisted?
Is the tier structure priced and sized correctly against real usage?
What is the package defect rate when Logan is not the operator?
Does the platform hold up outside Virginia — AHJ coverage, UPPA posture, code libraries?
Will contractors pay list price after experiencing the product?

Question 5 is the only one that cannot be gamed by enthusiasm. It is the primary exit criterion.

5.2 Cohort design

Size: 8–12 companies. Not more. Beyond twelve, personal review of every package before carrier submission becomes impossible, and that review gate is the primary risk control.

Composition:

Segment	Count	Profile
Small	3–4	1–3 reps, $500K–2M revenue
Mid	4–5	5–15 reps, $2M–15M revenue
Large	2–3	20+ reps, $15M+ revenue

Selection criteria:

Located outside the DC/MD/VA metro — no direct competitors
Has an existing supplement process (a shop with no documentation discipline cannot evaluate whether ours is better)
Written commitment to run 10+ claims through the platform in 90 days
Willing to take weekly 30-minute calls
Operating in a state where AHJ sweep and UPPA review are complete, or accepting of a disabled code module

Pricing: $99/month or $500 flat for the 90-day window. Beta users must pay. Free beta users produce polite feedback and near-zero usage. Paid beta users complain, which is the entire point of the exercise.

5.3 Scope constraints — hard gates

Three constraints must be enforced before a single external package reaches a carrier:

1. No interior water sections until issue #249 merges. IICRC content currently flows into prompts unchecked. That is a compliance exposure that would carry another company's name and license number on the document. Disable the module at the tenant level, not by instruction.

2. AHJ coverage gates geography. The golden set is Virginia. Either restrict the cohort to swept states, or ship out-of-state tenants with the code module explicitly disabled and disclose why. Do not allow unswept jurisdictions to generate code-citation content.

3. Multi-state UPPA review before external use. Lane discipline is architected to Virginia's statute (§ 38.2-1845.1 et seq.). Florida, Texas, Colorado, and other storm states operate under materially different public adjusting regimes. Obtain counsel review of the multi-state posture — and of liability allocation for AI-generated forensic documents used by third-party contractors — before the first out-of-state package is submitted. This was already logged as an open Phase 4 item; the beta is where it becomes concrete.

5.4 The review gate

Days 1–30: every proof package is reviewed by Logan before submission. Not optional, not sampled. Implement as a beta_hold state in the pipeline so the control is structural rather than procedural.

Days 31–90: sample review at 1-in-5. Track defect rate as the graduation metric.

The rationale is not product polish. A defective package does not cost a bug report — it costs a real contractor a real claim and a real homeowner a roof. That exposure must be actively managed, not accepted.

5.5 Instrumentation

Instrument these; do not rely on interviews alone.

Metric	What it validates
Packages per user per month	Tier volume assumptions
Time to first package	The 48-hour onboarding promise
% of packages generated by reps vs. owner	The core value proposition
Wizard abandonment by step	Field UX failure points
Edit rate on Fable drafts	Draft quality, measured not asked
Manifest swap / skip rate	AI slot-ranking accuracy
Seats activated vs. licensed	Seat banding validity
Storage consumed per tenant per month	COGS trajectory

Supplement outcomes	Track internally — never market (see §3.6)

Qualitative cadence: weekly 30-minute call per account, plus one shared Slack or Discord channel for the full cohort. Cross-pollination between beta users generates better product insight than individual calls and seeds the first referral network.

5.6 Exit criteria

Define before emotional investment in shipping. All six must clear:

 scripts/seed-acceptance-claim.ts runs green across all beta tenants
 Package defect rate under 5% on sampled review
 ≥60% of packages generated by non-owner users
 Median time-to-first-package under 72 hours
 ≥6 of 12 beta accounts convert to paid at list pricing
 Multi-state UPPA counsel review complete and documented

6.1 Corporate hygiene checklist — before first external sale
 RoofTrax formed as a distinct entity with its own EIN, banking, and accounting
 IP assignment executed — platform, codebase, price book logic, AHJ library, and document templates formally owned by RoofTrax, not NuHome. This is the item most often skipped and most expensive to fix later, particularly under any future diligence.
 Written services or licensing agreement governing anything shared between the two companies, at arm's-length terms
 NuHome executes a standard customer agreement on standard terms — no free perpetual access, no informal arrangement
 Tenant isolation verified and documented; contractual non-use terms drafted for all customer agreements
 Independent RoofTrax brand assets, domain, and email — no NuHome letterhead or license numbers in any RoofTrax material
 Separate E&O and cyber coverage written for a software company, not a contractor
 Founder time allocation between entities documented

The largest single lever in the entire model is the annual prepay rate. Every point it moves funds acquisition without external financing. In a business with ~96% gross margins, growth is constrained not by capital but by onboarding throughput — which makes the migration-performed-for-you promise both a sales asset and the primary operational bottleneck to solve before scale.
