## Class 1 — Elevation / Establishing
Pattern: `{Elevation} elevation, {property address or "subject property"}{, context note}.`
- "Front elevation, subject property. Two-story townhome; interior unit with party walls at both sides."
- "Rear elevation. Vinyl-sided upper story above brick; damage locations documented in Sections {n}."
Use: establishes the property and orientation. Context notes limited to recorded structure facts.

## Class 2 — Collateral / Soft Metal
Pattern: `{Component}, {elevation/location}: {documented condition}{; recency/direction observation as recorded}.`
- "Aluminum downspout, west elevation: impact indentations across the exposed face."
- "Window screen, south elevation: impact tears at two locations, documented during ground-level collateral sweep."
Use: exposure indicators on accessory components. State the condition; the inferential weight (event occurred on site) belongs to the report body, not the caption.

## Class 3 — Spatter
Pattern: `{Surface}, {location}: documented spatter marks{, orientation as recorded}. The field record identifies the observed orientation for comparison with other documented conditions.`
- "Oxidized electric meter cover, east elevation: documented spatter marks, tails oriented downward-left as recorded. The field record identifies the observed orientation for comparison with other documented conditions."
Rule: never reference spatter dimensions in relation to hail size, and never caption the image itself as a timing or causation conclusion — the orientation observation is recorded; its significance is analyzed in the report body.

## Class 4 — Roof Damage Close-Up
Pattern: `{Facet ID / slope}, {component}: {documented condition}, {marking/scale reference}.`

- "Facet R-1 (front slope): granule displacement with exposed asphalt at impact point; scale reference in frame."
Rule: condition adjectives and confirmation references appear only where the record contains the supporting observation or method, and referenced tests are attributed to the field record — never represented as visually established by the photograph.

## Class 5 — Test Square
Pattern: `Facet {ID}, test square {n} ({dimensions}): {count} {condition type} within marked area; chalked prior to photograph.`
- "Facet R-2, test square 1 (10' × 10'): eight impact points with granule displacement within marked area; chalked prior to photograph."
Rule: "chalked prior to photograph" is standing language — it documents the marking sequence in the exhibit itself.

## Class 6 — Comparison Set (vertical stack, one page)
Structure: one **set caption** above or below the pair + one caption per photo stating precisely what that photo contributes.
Set pattern: `Comparison — {condition A} (top) and {condition B} (bottom), {shared location context}.`
Per-photo pattern: `{Position}: {facet/location} — {exactly what this photo shows and why it sits in this comparison}.`

Example — recency comparison:
- Set: "Comparison — impact points documented at Facet R-2: impact point with documented recent-character indicators (top) and condition documented as pre-existing (bottom)."

- Bottom: "Condition documented as pre-existing, with weathered characteristics noted in the field record: granule loss with weathered margins and grayed, exposed mat fibers."

Example — cause differentiation:
- Set: "Comparison — localized impact condition (top) and general surface weathering (bottom), Facet R-4."

- Bottom: "General weathering: uniform granule thinning across the field, documented as age-related surface wear."

Rule: each photo's caption must make the comparison legible on its own — a reader seeing only one photo and its caption should know what it depicts and which side of the comparison it carries.

# Inspection Method & Evidence Framework

## Uniform Inspection Procedure

{{company_name}} performs every property inspection in the same fixed sequence, regardless of property, peril, or claim status. The sequence is: (1) policyholder interview; (2) ground-level exterior walk beginning at the front elevation and proceeding clockwise, documenting each elevation and its soft-metal and accessory components; (3) roof access and slope-by-slope examination in a fixed order, with chalk-marked test areas on each slope; (4) attic and interior examination where interior conditions are reported or suspected; (5) contemporaneous field notes and an end-of-inspection written synopsis recorded before leaving the property.

Because the procedure does not vary, the photographic record produced at each inspection is internally auditable: the order and coverage of the photo set itself demonstrates that the full property was examined, not only the areas where conditions were found.

## Field Documentation Protocol

All observations are documented at the moment of capture. Conditions are identified in-frame (chalk marking, measurement reference, or written note photographed with the condition) rather than characterized afterward from memory. Photographs are retained at full original resolution in a digital index. Field notes are timestamped, and an inspection synopsis is written on-site at the conclusion of the inspection, creating a contemporaneous written record of findings as they were observed.

## Policyholder Interview

Before the physical inspection, {{company_name}} interviews the policyholder to establish reported history relevant to the construction assessment, including: when interior symptoms (if any) were first observed; the date and nature of the last known roof or exterior work; any prior repairs, claims, or known events at the property; and the age of the existing systems where known. Interview responses are recorded in the inspection file and are identified in this report as reported history, distinguished from directly observed conditions.

## Examination Method and Controls

The examination applies a differential method. Damage attribution is tested — not assumed — by comparing conditions across areas of differing exposure:

- Conditions are documented on all slopes and elevations, including those where no damage is expected, so that the distribution of findings can be evaluated against the reported event rather than sampled selectively.
- Sheltered and shielded areas (wind-shadowed zones, overhang-protected wall sections, leeward faces) are examined as lower-exposure reference areas. A storm-related condition is expected to appear at materially lower density in these areas — reduced, though not necessarily absent, since wind vortices can carry impact into partially sheltered zones.
- Test areas of fixed size are chalk-marked on each examined slope or elevation, and conditions within each test area are counted and photographed, so that density and extent are measured rather than characterized.
- Conditions unrelated to the reported event (age-related wear, mechanical damage, animal activity, installation deficiencies, prior repairs) are documented and identified as such wherever observed. Identifying unrelated conditions is part of the examination, not an exception to it: distinguishing them is what isolates the event-related findings.

## Expected Indicators

The examination evaluates observed conditions against the physical indicators that the reported event type would be expected to produce on the installed materials. The indicators applied in this report:

## Definition of Damage

<!-- [+] Rationale sentence: why this definition applies (training: policies rarely define physical damage; the applied standard is the court-recognized one) -->
The term "damage" as used in this package refers to a direct, distinct, demonstrable, and detrimental physical change to tangible property — the standard recognized in construction and forensic practice for evaluating physical loss. Each element is applied as follows:

- **Direct:** The damage must be a straightforward, unbroken result of a specific identified peril (such as hail, wind, or fire) rather than a pre-existing issue or gradual wear and tear.
- **Distinct:** The damage must be localized and clearly distinguishable from the surrounding, unaffected areas of the property.
- **Demonstrable:** The physical loss or alteration must be clearly visible, measurable, or capable of being proven through photographic or objective evidence.
- **Detrimental:** The altered condition must have a negative impact, meaning it has compromised the property's structural integrity, value, or intended function.

Conditions documented in this package are identified as damage only where all four elements are satisfied by the recorded evidence.

**Code Upgrade** refers to code-required work that goes beyond restoring the damaged component to its pre-loss configuration, including the addition or modification of components not previously installed, where a verified current-code provision requires that work because of the documented repair. A Code Upgrade is not elective work; it is separately identified because it results from a code requirement beyond simple like-for-like restoration.

Each Code Compliance or Code Upgrade item in this package is supported by the applicable code citation, factual trigger, repair-scope connection, and supporting documentation.

## Purpose

Identification of the installed roofing product can bear on the repairability of the roof covering. Where the installed product is discontinued, individually damaged units cannot be replaced with the same product, and the repair analysis evaluates whether an available alternative can be integrated with the installed system while preserving required dimensions, profile, weather-shedding function, fastening, appearance where relevant, and applicable manufacturer or construction requirements.

This section describes {{company_name}}'s method for that evaluation. It consists of three separate determinations, made in order: identification of the installed product; determination of that product's discontinued status; and assessment of the compatibility of available alternatives. No one of these determinations is inferred from another.

## Determination 1 — Identification Classification

Product identification begins with a field classification based on documented observable characteristics. The inspector classifies the product as **Identified** or **Lab Identification Recommended**:

- **Identified:** The documented physical characteristics sufficiently support a specific product identification and distinguish it from reasonable alternatives in the available reference sources. An Identified classification may support product-specific analysis.
- **Lab Identification Recommended:** The available field evidence does not support a product identification to that standard. Laboratory identification or other qualified product verification is recommended and recorded, and no product-specific conclusion relies on a field identification. Observations regarding candidate products may be recorded as inspection notes; such notes are not identifications and support no determination.

### Comparison Method

Classification is performed by physical comparison of documented field characteristics against {{company_name}}'s product reference library. The comparison considers the applicable observable characteristics available at the property, which may include:

- Product profile and cutout geometry
- Overall dimensions, exposure, and thickness
- Lamination configuration or tab pattern
- Surfacing and granule pattern
- Backside markings, wrapper, bundle, invoice, or permit information
- Manufacturer logos or embossed identifiers
- Date-of-installation information, where documented
- Product sample or laboratory result, where obtained

The candidate identification is evaluated for consistency with documented field measurements and observable characteristics. Exposure and product length are corroborating characteristics; they are not treated as independent manufacturer or laboratory confirmation. Where the documented characteristics do not sufficiently distinguish the candidate from reasonable alternatives, the product is not classified as Identified; laboratory identification or other qualified product verification is recommended and recorded.

### Basis and Limits of the Classification

The classification reflects the degree to which the documented field characteristics support the stated identification within the available reference sources. It is not represented as manufacturer confirmation, laboratory confirmation, or an exhaustive exclusion of every possible alternative unless separately supported by qualified verification. Where the distinction is material to a determination in this report, the basis of the classification is stated alongside the determination it supports.

## Determination 2 — Discontinued-Status Determination

Product discontinuation is evaluated separately from product identification, and discontinued status is recorded separately from availability: a discontinued product may remain available through distributor inventory, reclaimed stock, or other sources, and the two findings rest on different evidence. Where either finding is material to the analysis, the inspection record identifies the product, the source of the determination, the source date, and the basis for the recorded status — for discontinuation, that the product is no longer manufactured; for availability, whether the product remains reasonably obtainable through ordinary distribution channels. A product identified as discontinued is not automatically deemed nonrepairable; compatibility of available alternatives must be evaluated separately.

## Determination 3 — Repair-Compatibility Assessment

Where repair (rather than replacement) of the covering is under evaluation and the installed product is discontinued or otherwise unavailable, the analysis assesses whether an available alternative can be physically integrated with the installed system. The assessment identifies the compatibility criteria applied — which may include dimensions, exposure, profile and interlock geometry, weather-shedding function, fastening requirements, appearance where relevant, and applicable manufacturer or construction requirements — and the evidence evaluated against each criterion. No available product is characterized as compatible or incompatible except by reference to the documented criteria and evidence.

# Repairability Assessment Field Protocol — v1.1
<!-- BOILERPLATE MODULE — Section: repairability_field_protocol -->
<!-- Classification: [BP] per-tenant, VERSION-CONTROLLED. Reports cite this document by name and version (STD-RPR-INT-01). -->
<!-- Template variables: {{company_name}} -->
<!-- Alignment reference: STD-RPR-01 (Williams, JNAFE 37(1) 2020) via the STD-RPR-01 Source Record. Deviations from the published method are recorded in §9 — never left implicit. -->

## 1. Gate and Scope

The repairability assessment is performed only where recorded as **warranted and authorized** at Stage 7 of the Uniform Inspection Procedure. Where the assessment is not performed, the gate reason is recorded (Not Warranted — Discontinued; Not Authorized), and no repairability conclusion is drawn from an unperformed assessment. The assessment supplements the damage documentation completed in Stage 6; it is never performed in place of it (Source Record P12).

Assessment target (roof or siding) and covering type are recorded. This protocol version covers **asphalt shingle** roof coverings; other covering types proceed under their own protocol modules when published.

## 2. Conditions of Assessment

Environmental conditions at the assessment are those recorded in the Stage 2 Arrival Record (temperature, wind, sky conditions). The published method's environmental windows (air temperature 40–90°F; wind gusts under 25 mph; dry surfaces; no imminent precipitation; in-season testing preferred — Source Record P6) are the reference conditions. Where the recorded conditions fall outside those windows, the assessment record notes the condition and its bearing on the results, consistent with the published method's off-season guidance that testing mirror the conditions of the anticipated repair.

## 3. Test Area Selection

The subject shingle is an **already-damaged field shingle** wherever available — a shingle with documented damage attributed to the reported event — on a slope where damage has been identified (Source Record P5). Where no damaged shingle is usable, the assessment is performed on a slope with identified damage, and the basis is recorded with a note (the published method's stated fallback).

Selection criteria are confirmed individually in the field application before marking begins, and the confirmations are transmitted with the assessment record: subject shingle full-length and uncut; at least two courses above any eave (a minimum, not a fixed position); at least one full shingle length from any rake, valley, or hip; no vents, penetrations, or appurtenances within the assessment area; and an assessment area representative of the overall roof exposure (not sheltered by trees or adjacent structures). Where roof surfaces include shingles of different types or installation dates, an assessment is performed for each damaged type.

## 4. Marking and Baseline (RAP1)

1. Identify the damaged field shingle to be pulled and mark it "X."
2. Mark the shingles directly below X as **1 and 2**.
3. Mark the shingles left and right of X as **3 and 4**.
4. Mark the two shingles above X as **5 and 6**.
5. Mark the shingles above 5 and 6 as **7 and 8**, where the layout requires their manipulation.
6. Record the number of shingles requiring manipulation to complete the protocol (**6, 7, or 8**).
7. Mark any pre-existing damage within the assessment area in a distinct, distinguishable manner before any manipulation, so that pre-existing conditions are separated from assessment-induced conditions in the photographic record.
8. Photograph the marked assessment area (**RAP1** — post-markup, pre-manipulation baseline).

## 5. Pull Sequence

1. Break all seals securing the assessment area using a standard flat bar or 5-in-1 painter's tool.
2. Locate and gently remove all nails fastening shingle X.
3. Pull shingle X.
4. Record whether **Shingle 1** and **Shingle 2** each sustained mat transfer during the removal process (individually determined, Yes/No).
5. While X is removed, observe and photograph the exposed underlayment at the removal location; record any damage attributable to the removal process.

## 6. Replace and Re-Secure

1. Replace shingle X to its original location.
2. Without over-lifting the surrounding shingles, properly re-nail all removed fasteners in **new hole locations**, straight and flush.
3. Verify re-seating by hand-tapping the surface of all manipulated shingles at approximately six-inch intervals (by hand only — no tool).

*Conditional variant — sample retention:* where laboratory product identification has been recommended (product_id_class = lab_recommended) and a manufacturer-compliant replacement shingle is available on site, shingle X may instead be **retained as the laboratory sample** and a new shingle installed and secured per the manufacturer's instructions. The variant used is recorded. (Aligns with Source Record P13 and the published method's step 8.)

## 7. Outcome Determination

For each condition question, an affirmative answer opens per-shingle selection (shingles 3–8; shingles 1 and 2 carry their own mat-transfer determinations in §5), one example photograph, and a photo note identifying the shingle and the observed condition:

1. **Delamination** sustained by any shingle (laminate sheet separation).
2. **Creasing, cracking, or fracturing** sustained by any shingle.
3. **Nail pull-through or nail-zone damage** sustained by any shingle.
4. **Puncture, tearing, or gouging** sustained while seals were being released.
5. **Failure to re-seat flat or to be properly re-secured** after replacement of X.

Pre-existing conditions marked under §4.7 are excluded from all outcome determinations; only conditions arising from the assessment are recorded as outcomes (Source Record P4).

## 8. Scorecard and Derived Measures

The scorecard records: manipulated-shingle count; new collateral-damaged shingle count; mat-transfer findings on shingles 1–2; and per-condition counts for delamination, creasing/cracking/fracture, nail-zone damage, puncture/tear/gouge, and re-seating/seal-integration concerns.

Derived at processing (Members application), consistent with the published method's reporting definitions:

- **Damage rate** — the number of *unique* shingles (1–8) that sustained any assessment-induced condition, counted once per shingle regardless of how many condition types it sustained (Source Record P4). Damage to shingle X itself is documented where noteworthy but excluded from the rate.
- **Damage ratio** — the damage rate over the number of shingles manipulated, reported as "(rate) to (manipulated count)."
- **Total repairability assessment score** — the damage rate plus the general-conditions score (deterioration, installation errors, prior repairs, manufacturing defects, material obsolescence, visual incompatibility — scored not-present/present/limiting from the attested inspection record, per Source Record P10), evaluated against the published thresholds. The general-conditions inputs are drawn from the Stage 6 record and the product-identification record; they are not re-judged at the roof edge.

## 9. Recorded Deviations from the Published Method
*(per the Source Record's rule: deviations are recorded, not left implicit)*

1. **Personnel.** The published method contemplates a licensed professional engineer as evaluator with an independent roofing contractor performing manipulations. This protocol is performed by {{company_name}}'s credentialed inspector as a contractor assessment. Reports cite alignment with the method's mechanics, never performance under the published method (Source Record, Alignment Constraint 1).
2. **Reinstatement of X.** The published method installs a new manufacturer-compliant shingle. This protocol's default reinstates the original shingle X with fasteners in new hole locations, returning the roof to an as-found configuration without introducing repair materials; the new-shingle path is the conditional variant in §6. The default is the less invasive procedure for an assessment performed before any repair contract exists.
3. **Manipulated count.** The published method fixes the primary damage assessment area at eight perimeter shingles; this protocol records the actual manipulated count (6–8) as the layout requires, and reports the damage ratio against that recorded count.
4. **Condition-type capture.** The published method strikes shingle numbers and counts struck shingles; this protocol captures per-condition, per-shingle outcomes (richer record), from which the published per-shingle damage rate is derived without double counting.
5. **Re-seat verification.** The six-inch hand-tap verification in §6.3 is a protocol addition not present in the published method.

## 10. Report Outputs

The scorecard, the RAP1 baseline photograph, and up to two newly-damaged-shingle photographs are carried into the report — priority to one delamination example and one creasing/cracking/fracture example. Damage rate, damage ratio, and the total score render with their derivations. The report cites this protocol by name and version, and cites STD-RPR-01 through its Source Record locators for the methodological propositions applied.

# Uniform Inspection Procedure

{{company_name}} conducts every property inspection under the same two-phase procedure, using a structured field application that timestamps each record at the moment of capture and anchors the inspection to the property by GPS. The stages of the procedure are always performed in the same fixed order; which stages are performed is determined by the property's documented conditions, not by inspector discretion. System-specific examinations (siding, interior) are conducted when damage to that system is reported or was identified at the preliminary inspection, and are omitted when it is not — the scope of each forensic inspection is therefore set by the damage locations recorded in Phase I and confirmed at ground level on arrival. The four-elevation photographic record and full-perimeter ground-level examination are performed at every property regardless of reported damage locations, and it is this whole-property baseline that establishes, on the record, which system examinations follow. Because stage order is fixed and enforced by the application, and stage scope is tied to documented conditions, the inspection record remains internally auditable: the record itself shows both what was examined and why.

## Phase I — Preliminary Inspection

A preliminary inspection is performed before any forensic documentation services are agreed to. Its purpose is limited: to confirm whether storm-related damage exists at the property and to identify the locations of damage (roof, siding, collateral, interior) that a forensic inspection would document. No forensic conclusions are drawn at this phase. If storm-related damage is not observed, the property owner is informed and no further services are proposed.

Forensic inspection services proceed only after the property owner has reviewed and executed a written pre-construction services agreement.

## Phase II — Forensic Inspection

### Stage 1 — Measurement Report Intake
A third-party aerial measurement report for the property is uploaded and parsed before field documentation begins. Roof and elevation geometry — facet inventory, areas, pitches, linear measurements, and facet orientations — auto-populate the corresponding inspection stages, so that field documentation is recorded against independently produced geometry rather than field-sketched approximations. Parsed values are confirmed by the inspector and verified against field conditions during the inspection.

### Stage 2 — Arrival Record
On arrival, the inspector logs: GPS-confirmed location, date and time, sky conditions, temperature, wind conditions, and the persons present. Sky and weather conditions are recorded because they bear on photographic conditions and on the day's examination methods.

### Stage 3 — Property Owner Interview *(conducted when the owner is present)*
Before physical examination, the inspector documents owner-reported property and loss history relevant to the inspection scope. The interview may address length of ownership; known age and maintenance history of roof and siding systems; whether the owner was present during the reported event; mitigation undertaken before inspection; reported prior losses or claims; prior repairs; and previously known unrepaired conditions. Owner statements are recorded as reported history and are distinguished from the inspector's observed conditions. Owner-reported information is not independently verified unless otherwise identified in the inspection record. The inspection protocol does not interpret policy coverage or make coverage determinations.

### Stage 4 — Property Profile
The structure is classified: property type (single-family detached, townhome, etc.), stories, roof geometry class, and accessibility conditions. These classifications drive the applicable documentation requirements for the remainder of the inspection (including party-wall and fire-separation documentation where the structure is attached).

### Stage 5 — Elevations and Ground-Level Examination
All four elevations are photographed in fixed order — front, right, rear, left — regardless of where damage is reported. Reported damage locations (roof, siding, collateral, interior) are confirmed or revised based on ground-level observation. A ground-level collateral sweep is then performed and documented, covering soft metals and accessory components (gutters, downspouts, screens, HVAC fins, fencing, and similar exposure indicators) on all elevations, including elevations where no damage is expected.

### Stage 6 — Roof Examination
The roof is examined and documented in the following fixed order:

1. **Eave and edge assembly.** At the eave: shingle pull-up photograph, shingle gauge photograph, and presence/absence determination (or documented as undetermined) for each edge component — gutter apron, drip edge, starter course, ice-and-water barrier, underlayment.
2. **Decking determination.** Where decking is observable, it is identified by type and thickness (3/8" plywood, 1/2" plywood, 7/16" OSB, spaced decking); where not observable, it is recorded as undetermined pending tear-off verification.
3. **Facet-by-facet examination.** Each roof facet, as inventoried by the measurement report, is examined individually and documented with: area, covering material, pitch, and applicable tie-in protocol (valley/hip/ridge); photographs of each observed damage condition on the facet; and separate photographs of pre-existing or unrelated conditions, identified as such. Damage and unrelated conditions are documented on every facet examined, including facets with no event-related findings.
4. **Whole-roof linears and overviews.** Ridge, hip, valley, eave, and rake linear measurements are confirmed, and ridge/hip overview photographs are captured.
5. **Penetrations.** All roof penetrations are documented, with storm-related damage annotated where present.
6. **Test squares** *(hail events)*. Test areas are chalk-marked first, then photographed as marked overviews, so that counted conditions are fixed in the photographic record at the moment of marking.
7. **Roofing product identification.** The inspector first answers a threshold question: can the installed product be identified with better than 50% certainty? If not, laboratory identification is recommended and recorded. If so, the product is selected from the company's discontinued-product reference library, and average exposure and product length are measured and confirmed against the identification.

### Stage 7 — Repairability Assessment Gate
The inspector records whether a repairability assessment is warranted and authorized at this time. Where an assessment is not warranted, the disqualifying condition is recorded (e.g., discontinued product; multiple existing covering layers). Where warranted and authorized, the examination proceeds under the company's repairability assessment protocol, documented separately.

### Stage 8 — Siding Examination *(where siding is within the reported or observed damage locations)*
Each siding facet, as inventoried by the measurement report, is examined individually: whether damage is present; damage type where present (wind, hail, tree/impact); photographs of each damage condition; whether the facet is isolated for repair purposes; pre-existing or unrelated conditions, identified as such; and a facet overview photograph. Each component of each siding facet is documented and classified for the corrective method it requires (detach-and-reset or remove-and-replace). The presence or absence of a water-resistive barrier is determined and recorded.

### Stage 9 — Interior Examination *(where interior conditions are reported or observed)*
Interior conditions are documented and physically traced to their exterior origin through the assembly path, so that interior findings are connected to the documented exterior condition rather than attributed by proximity.

### Stage 10 — Inspector Attestation
At the conclusion of the inspection, the inspector attests to the record: that the inspection was performed under this procedure, that the documented observations were made by the attesting inspector on the recorded date, and that findings are stated to the inspector's professional judgment within a reasonable degree of certainty. The attestation is recorded with the inspector's name and credentials.

## Record Handling

The complete inspection record — all photographs at original resolution, structured findings, measurements, and notes — is transmitted from the field application at the conclusion of the inspection for processing. No field records are altered after attestation; subsequent analysis and reporting are performed from, and cite back to, the attested field record.

### DET-AS-01 — Granule displacement (impact)

**Statement**

Granules are the shingle's mineral wearing surface, shielding the asphalt beneath from ultraviolet exposure. Where impact has displaced granules and exposed asphalt, the affected area has lost part of that protective surfacing, and the exposed asphalt weathers without it for the remaining service of the shingle; displaced granules do not re-adhere. The significance of the exposure to the product's listed performance — including any fire-resistance or weathering listing — is evaluated against the identified product's specifications and applicable listings.

**Required Support**

Product identification (or system reference) before any assertion regarding fire classification or listed performance; listing or product literature citation where such performance is discussed.

**Limitation**

Granule displacement alone does not establish concealed mat damage, active leakage, or the extent of affected material beyond what is documented.

### DET-AS-02 — Mat fracture / bruising (impact)

**Statement**

The reinforcing mat is the shingle's structural core. A confirmed fracture or bruise of the mat reduces the shingle's structural continuity at that location, and the affected area may worsen under thermal cycling and wind flexure, as the fracture is subject to propagation under repeated load. A mat compromised at an impact point no longer performs as the intact product was designed to perform at that location, even where the surface appears superficially intact.

**Required Support**

The identification method for the fracture/bruise stated in the record (what was felt, seen, or tested); technical reference where propagation or long-term consequence is asserted beyond the observed condition.

**Limitation**

An impact mark without a confirmed mat effect does not establish fracture; this entry does not establish leakage or present water entry.

---

### DET-AS-03 — Crease (wind)

**Statement**

A crease is a fold line formed where wind has displaced the shingle against its own plane, and it may indicate fracture or loss of material continuity along the fold. Where fracture at the crease is confirmed, the affected shingle no longer lies and loads as originally installed: the creased portion hinges at the fold under subsequent wind exposure, and the fracture does not re-fuse.

**Required Support**

Confirmation method in the field record before rendering the confirmed-fracture form; otherwise render the conditional form.

**Limitation**

A crease alone, without confirmation, does not establish mat fracture, permanent loss of wind resistance, or leakage.

### DET-AS-04 — Broken sealant bond (wind)

**Statement**

The factory sealant strip bonds each course to the one below and contributes materially to the covering's resistance to wind uplift and wind-driven rain; fasteners restrain the shingle, and the bond restrains its edges. Where the bond is broken, the unbonded edge can lift at lower wind exposure than the intact assembly and can admit wind-driven water beneath the covering at that edge.

**Required Support**

For any assertion attributing the broken bonds to a directional wind event rather than adhesive aging: the documented distribution pattern (bond condition by slope and orientation, including slopes where bonds were intact) must support the distinction, and the pattern evidence must be cited with the assertion.

**Limitation**

Broken bonds alone do not establish cause; aging, installation, and thermal history are among the mechanisms that can produce bond failure, and distribution evidence is required before cause is distinguished.

### DET-AS-05 — Lifted or displaced shingle

**Statement**

A shingle lifted or displaced from its designed position does not shed water as installed: displacement interrupts the coverage relationship between courses and can expose fastener penetrations and underlayment that the covering is designed to protect. Where the record documents displaced or pulled fasteners, enlarged fastener holes, or a shingle that cannot be re-seated to its designed position, the attachment at that location is also compromised.

**Required Support**

Fastener-related consequences rendered only where fastener displacement, enlarged holes, or failed re-seating is documented.

**Limitation**

Displacement alone does not establish fastener damage or leakage; a displaced shingle's bond and fastening condition are as documented, not presumed.

### DET-AS-06 — Fastener pull-through / withdrawal (wind)

**Statement**

Where wind loading has pulled the shingle over the fastener head or withdrawn the fastener, the designed attachment at that point no longer exists, and the shingle's remaining fasteners carry load the attachment pattern did not allocate to them. Where the pull-through leaves an exposed opening in the shingle, or prevents normal refastening at the designed location, the condition is both an attachment failure and a breach of the water-shedding plane at that point.

**Required Support**

The open-penetration consequence rendered only where the opening is documented.

**Limitation**

A pull-through does not by itself establish leakage to the interior or deck damage at the penetration.

### DET-AS-07 — Torn or missing shingle / tab

**Statement**

Where covering material is torn away or missing, the underlayment or deck at that location is directly exposed to weather and the covering's water-shedding function is absent at the loss. Edges of adjacent shingles are exposed to wind at boundaries the intact assembly did not present, a condition relevant to the loss's tendency to extend under subsequent wind exposure.

**Required Support**

None beyond the documented loss for the exposure statement; extension/propagation framed as tendency, not certainty.

**Limitation**

The documented loss establishes exposure at the loss; interior wetting or deck deterioration must be separately documented.

### DET-AS-08 — Wind-removed roof accessory / created opening

**Statement**

Where wind has removed an accessory and the assembly at its mount is opened or disturbed, the roof system's continuity is breached at that location to the depth documented — covering, underlayment, and, where mount penetrations are involved, the deck. A documented breach of the envelope admits water to the assembly with each wetting event until it is closed, and interior conditions traced to the breach share its cause.

**Required Support**

The opening documented photographically; interior attribution supported by the traced moisture path in the record.

**Limitation**

The breach establishes the entry point; the extent of interior effect is as traced and documented, not presumed.

### DET-CS-01 — Fresh split (impact or wind)

**Statement**

A split shake no longer sheds water as a single unit: the split is an opening in the shake's coverage, exposing the interlayment and fastening beneath it to water at that line, and the split halves move independently under wind and thermal load. Freshly exposed wood within the split — distinguishable from aged splits by its unweathered interior faces — absorbs water preferentially at the opening.

**Required Support**

The fresh/aged distinction documented in the record where the split's recency is asserted.

**Limitation**

A split establishes the opening; decay, leakage, and service-life effect are not established by the split alone.

### DET-CS-02 — Impact crush / fiber bruising

**Statement**

Where crushing of the wood surface is identified by a documented method, the compressed area's capacity to shed rather than absorb water is reduced at that location, and preferential water retention at a crushed area is a recognized contributor to localized deterioration in wood roofing materials.

**Required Support**

The identification method stated in the record; a wood-material or industry technical reference where service-life consequence is asserted.

**Limitation**

Surface marks without a documented identification method do not establish fiber crushing; this entry does not establish decay or a quantified life reduction.

---

### DET-IN-01 — Wetted insulation

**Statement**

Insulation performs thermally by trapping air within its structure, and wetting displaces that function: wetted insulation may experience reduced thermal performance, and wetting can compress loose-fill and batt structure. Recoverability on drying depends on the material type, the water exposure, and the material's documented condition; retained moisture within insulation also holds adjacent framing and finishes wet, extending the assembly's drying. Evaluation and disposition of wetted insulation follow the applicable water-damage restoration standard for the material and water category documented.

**Required Support**

Insulation type and observed/measured condition in the record; restoration-standard citation (S500 entry — human-entered locator) where disposition is stated.

**Limitation**

Wetting alone does not establish permanent performance loss for every material; the documented type, condition, and standard govern.

---

### DET-IN-02 — Water-affected gypsum drywall

**Statement**

Gypsum board is degraded by wetting in proportion to the exposure: wetting can soften the core, delaminate the paper facing, and reduce fastener holding at the affected area. Wetted gypsum is evaluated for loss of integrity, delamination, contamination, and restorability under the applicable water-damage restoration standard, considering the water category, duration, and the material's documented condition; material that cannot be restored to its pre-loss condition under that standard is removed and replaced.

**Required Support**

Documented condition observations; restoration-standard citation (S500 entry — human-entered locator) where the evaluation criteria or disposition are stated.

**Limitation**

Water contact alone does not establish that all affected board requires removal; the documented evaluation under the standard governs the disposition.

---

### DET-IN-03 — Wetted structural sheathing / decking

**Statement**

Wood structural panels are subject to edge swelling, glue-line delamination, and reduced fastener holding from water exposure. Where the record documents such deterioration — swelling, delamination, decay, or measured degradation — the affected panel no longer provides the solid, nailable substrate that covering attachment requires at that area, and the deteriorated area is corrected before new covering is installed. Deck condition concealed by the covering is confirmed at tear-off, and the record is supplemented with the exposed conditions.

**Required Support**

The documented deterioration observation (or tear-off confirmation) before the substrate-consequence statement is rendered.

**Limitation**

A wetting event alone does not establish panel deterioration; undetermined deck condition is recorded as undetermined pending exposure.

---

### DET-SM-01 — Impact indentation (metal)

**Statement**

An impact indentation is a permanent deformation: the metal has yielded and does not recover its formed geometry. Where inspection confirms fracture of the finish or exposed substrate at the deformation, the panel's factory corrosion protection is breached at that point, and the breach is the location from which corrosion of the substrate can initiate. Where indentations alter panel geometry at seams, ribs, or drainage planes, the effect on the panel's designed drainage or engagement at those locations is evaluated and documented.

**Required Support**

Coating fracture / substrate exposure confirmed and documented before any corrosion-related assertion; geometry effects at seams or drainage documented where asserted.

**Limitation**

An indentation alone does not establish coating fracture, corrosion initiation, or functional impairment; those conditions are confirmed, not inferred.

---

### DET-SM-02 — Disengaged seam / clip (wind)

**Statement**

In a standing seam system the seam is both the structural connection and the waterproofing joint. A documented disengaged seam or clip no longer restrains the panel as designed and no longer closes the joint at the disengagement: the panel can work under wind load at the open connection, and the open seam can admit wind-driven water. Panel systems are engineered as continuous assemblies, and a disengaged connection shifts uplift demand to adjacent connections beyond the design's allocation at that location.

**Required Support**

None beyond the documented disengagement; system-level engineering statements kept to the design principle stated.

**Limitation**

A disengagement establishes the open connection; water entry and adjacent-connection damage are as separately documented.

---

### DET-SM-03 — Finish fracture / coating breach

**Statement**

The factory-applied coating is the panel's corrosion protection, and a documented breach through to substrate is the point at which that protection is absent. Corrosion of exposed substrate initiates at such a breach and can undercut adjacent finish over time. Whether field-applied touch-up constitutes an accepted repair of the identified panel's coating system is determined by the panel manufacturer's published repair and finish-warranty position, which is cited where this determination bears on the corrective scope.

**Required Support**

Manufacturer repair/warranty position for the identified panel and finish, cited wherever touch-up adequacy is addressed.

**Limitation**

A coating breach does not establish present corrosion beyond what is observed, nor the manufacturer's position absent the citation.

---

### DET-VS-01 — Vinyl siding impact fracture

**Statement**

A fractured vinyl panel cannot be restored to an intact, continuous panel: vinyl does not rejoin, and patching does not reconstitute the panel's continuous surface. The fracture is an opening in the cladding at that panel, can admit wind-driven water behind the cladding plane at the break, and is subject to propagation from the crack tips under thermal movement. Where the fracture affects the panel's weather-shedding function, integrity, or appearance, correction is by replacement of the affected panel.

**Required Support**

None for the statement as written; propagation asserted as susceptibility, not schedule.

**Limitation**

A panel fracture does not by itself establish wetting of the water-resistive barrier or sheathing; effects behind the cladding depend on the assembly and are as documented.

---

### DET-VS-02 — Unlocked / displaced panel

**Statement**

A panel unseated from its interlock is not restrained as designed: the open lock can admit wind and wind-driven water behind the cladding, and an unrestrained panel works under wind load, stressing its remaining engagement and fastening. Where the record documents deformation of the locking profile, re-seating does not restore the designed engagement, and correction is by replacement of the affected panel; where no lock deformation is documented, the panel's capacity to re-engage is as observed in the field record.

**Required Support**

Deformation documented (photograph or noted failed re-engagement) before the deformation consequence is rendered.

**Limitation**

Simple unseating does not establish lock damage; the two conditions are documented and treated separately.

---

### DET-VS-03 — Puncture / debris impact

**Statement**

A puncture is an opening in the cladding at the panel and can admit wind-driven water behind the siding at that point. The consequence of water behind the cladding depends on the assembly: where a water-resistive barrier is present and intact, the barrier is the next plane of protection; where the record documents no barrier, water entering at the puncture can reach the sheathing directly. The assembly condition behind the cladding is as documented in the inspection record, including the recorded presence or absence of a water-resistive barrier.

**Required Support**

The WRB determination from the field record (present / absent / undetermined) accompanies any statement about what lies behind the cladding.

**Limitation**

A puncture does not by itself establish wetting or deterioration of the barrier or sheathing.
